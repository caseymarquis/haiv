**File:** `worktrees/main/haiv-lib/src/haiv/paths.py`

## Why I came here

I needed to see `AtlasPaths` before deciding where project-local examples should live. The atlas path structure determines what's a natural fit.

## What I found

`paths.py` is the entire geography of haiv in one file. Everything — project root, users, minds, packages, atlas — gets a dataclass with `@property` accessors. Clean naming convention: `*_dir` for directories, `*_file` for files, no suffix for container objects.

**AtlasPaths** (lines 270-309) is simple:
- `root` → `atlas/`
- `welcome_file` → `atlas/welcome.md`
- `quests_file` → `atlas/quests.md`
- `journeys_dir` → `atlas/journeys/`
- `maps_dir` → `atlas/maps/`
- `eras_dir` → `atlas/journeys/eras/`

No examples concept yet. Adding one is trivial — just another `@property`. The question is *where* it should point.

**Design decision — where should examples live?**

Option A: `atlas/examples/` — a peer of `journeys/` and `maps/`. This treats examples as their own atlas concept.

Option B: `atlas/journeys/examples/` — nested under journeys, since the examples *are* journeys. This is more semantically accurate: an example journey is a journey that happens to serve as a teaching example.

I lean toward Option B. An example journey is a journey. Putting it under `journeys/` means it's discoverable alongside other journeys. It also means `atlas/journeys/` is the single place to look for anything journey-shaped. But there's a concern: would it show up in regular journey listings and confuse things? The `eras/` folder already lives under `journeys/` as a special subfolder, so there's precedent for special-purpose directories there.

**Something I appreciated:** The whole file is dataclasses with properties. No methods that do work, no I/O, no side effects. Pure structure. Makes it safe to extend — adding a property can't break anything.
