# 005 — The Welcome Template

**File:** `worktrees/main/haiv-core/src/haiv_core/__assets__/minds/welcome.md.j2`

## Why I came here

Autonomous mode needs a different welcome template — one that encourages independent decision-making instead of collaboration. I need to see the current template to understand what to change.

## What I found

54 lines. Two sections: assignment structure (filled in by the creator) and "Before You Begin" (instructions for the mind).

**Assignment structure (lines 1-38):**
- Task title, description, location (conditional on `{{ location }}`), requirements, success criteria, verification commands, process steps
- This is filled in by the PM/creator, not the mind. It's a form.

**"Before You Begin" (lines 42-53):**
- Step 1: Read the assignment
- Step 2: Run `hv chart`
- Step 3: Decision point — explore if atlas is insufficient
- Step 4: **"Discuss your approach with your human collaborator"** — this is the collaborative assumption
- Footer: "Work collaboratively with your human. Do not use planning tools unless they explicitly request it."

**Template variables:** Only `{{ location }}` is used, with a conditional `{% if location %}` block.

## What this means for my task

The assignment structure (top half) is mode-agnostic — requirements, success criteria, verification all apply regardless. It's the "Before You Begin" section that needs an autonomous variant.

For autonomous mode, step 4 changes from "discuss with your human" to something like "make your own decisions and proceed." The footer changes from "work collaboratively" to "work independently."

**Two approaches:**
1. **Separate template** — `welcome-autonomous.md.j2` with different "Before You Begin" content. Simple, clear, no template logic.
2. **Conditional in one template** — `{% if mode == "autonomous" %}` blocks. Keeps everything in one file but adds complexity.

I lean toward a separate template. The templates are small (54 lines), the differences are meaningful, and having two files makes it obvious that two modes exist. The `scaffold_mind` function already takes a `location` string — it could also take a template name or mode flag.
